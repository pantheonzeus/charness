## 1. How recruiter search actually works (the whole point)

LinkedIn search behaves like Google: **pure keyword matching**. A headhunter's funnel is:

1. Type the **exact job title** they're hiring for.
2. Filter by **industry**.
3. Narrow with **specific hard skills / systems / software**.

Manual outreach comes off that shortlist. **If your profile does not contain the exact terms they type, you are invisible** — no matter how strong the career. Optimization = putting the right terms in the places the algorithm weights, in a way you can defend in an interview.

### Keyword placement priority (highest algorithm weight first)
1. **Headline** — the #1 place the algorithm looks.
2. **Skills** — the biggest *pure*-algorithm driver (up to 50; recruiters rarely read them, the algorithm always does).
3. **About** — the 2,000-character "keyword vault."
4. **Education / Certifications** — adds searchable terms ("MBA," "Gaia-X," "SAFe").

### Finding your keywords empirically
Paste 5–10 real target job descriptions into a word-counter; take the top 5–10 recurring **industry terms** and **hard skills**. Those are the terms recruiters type. Also use a target posting's **"Skills match" panel** to see the exact algorithm-weighted skills tied to that role — add any you legitimately hold.

## 2. Headline formula (220 characters)

**Never** use the default "Title at Company" (you blend in) or buzzwords ("visionary," "thought leader," "rock star" — nobody *searches* those).

**Primary formula:**
```
Target Job Title | Years of Experience in Target Sector | 3 high-priority keywords | Value proposition
```
- Value proposition = one sentence starting with **"helped/helping"** + a **quantified** achievement.

**Alternative (consultant / heavy-networker) — XYZ:**
```
[Title] helping [target companies] achieve [result] by [action]
```

## 3. About section (2,000 chars — your "cover letter")

Prime keyword real estate. **Kill the "here's me / here's what I want" framing** — frame around **solving the employer's problem**; answer the questions you get on screening calls.

**Template A — AIDA (default):**
- **Attention** — a bold opener: a metric, a sharp statement, or an emoji that stops the scroll.
- **Interest** — the specific roles, industries, technical skills, and systems you work in (dense with keywords).
- **Desire** — differentiating accomplishments + certs, each with a metric ("led a team that cut delivery time 30%").
- **Action** — an explicit CTA: message/email me; include contact details if you're not always active on LinkedIn.

**Template B — PAS (pain-led alternative):**
- **Pain** — name an industry pain point the employer feels.
- **Agitate** — the cost of not fixing it.
- **Solution** — your track record + metrics as the fix.
- **Action** — CTA.

## 4. Skills & keywords

- Up to **50 skills**. Recruiters distrust them (self-reported), but they are a **massive algorithm/SEO lever**.
- **Pin your top 5 hard skills** so they surface first — make them your highest-priority target keywords.
- **Skills-match hack:** on a target job posting, open the "Skills match" panel → add every high-priority skill you legitimately have. (Coach's anecdotal claim: +500%–5,000% profile views.)
- **Skill assessments:** passing earns a verified badge; failing stays private — no downside to trying.

## 5. Experience / accomplishments

- **Lead each role with company context** — size, industry, function ("Deep-tech scale-up, 100+ FTE, edge-to-cloud AI for manufacturing"). Recruiters want people used to *their* kind of environment.
- **Achievements over duties**, in scannable bullets, **each with a metric**. Recruiters skim fast.
- **Diversify metric types** (rotate them so it doesn't read as one note):
  - **Absolute** — team of 10, €300k budget, 100+ FTE.
  - **Ratio / proportion** — conversion 10%→15%, 5:1 ROI.
  - **Time-to-result** — cut delivery by 2 months *(the strongest "hit the ground running" signal)*.
  - **Ranking / rating** — top 10%, 4.9/5 CSAT.
  - **Growth** — +20% productivity, +100 accounts.
  - **Savings** — "saved €30k/yr optimizing supply chain."
- Update regularly — signals an active, current career.

## 6. Settings & polish

- **Open to Work → recruiters-only.** At senior level, **avoid the public green banner** — negativity bias makes hiring managers wonder why you haven't been snapped up. Use the hidden "Open to Work for recruiters only" (visible only to LinkedIn Recruiter accounts); set specific **target titles, locations, and role types** (this itself feeds recruiter search).
- **Turn OFF profile-update broadcasts BEFORE editing** — otherwise LinkedIn notifies your whole network, including your current employer. (Settings → Visibility → Share profile updates → Off.)
- **Custom URL** — clean it to `linkedin.com/in/firstname-lastname` (add middle initial / title / industry if taken). No nicknames, numbers, jokes, symbols. Marginal SEO gain; signals attention to detail.
- **Featured section** — pin proof: a flagship deck, an article, a talk. **Photo:** professional, approachable, current. **Banner:** communicate positioning, not a stock landscape.

---

## 7. Senior-level mistakes to avoid

- Public green Open-to-Work banner.
- Default or buzzword-stuffed headline.
- **Incompatible target titles** in one profile (e.g. "Director of Operations" next to "Operations Assistant") — signals you don't know what you want. Keep **one cohesive title story**.
- Self-centered About that ignores the employer's problem.
- Experience/banner as a duty laundry-list or generic titles ("Project Manager, Team Leader, Problem Solver") instead of quantified results.
- **Unproofed AI text** — AI hallucinates/alters real titles and context; recruiters bounce on awkward phrasing. Proof every line; keep your real titles exact.
- Messy URL; vague/outdated recommendations ("Bob is great" — no context, action, or result).
