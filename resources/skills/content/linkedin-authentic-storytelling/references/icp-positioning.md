Write from inside the European reality. **Sovereignty framing is also the highest-converting axis measured so far** (2.75% engagement, the Finnish Independence Day post — see `[[LinkedIn-Posts-Index]]`) and is under-used; lean into it deliberately, especially for the manufacturing segment where sovereignty is a capacity/resilience argument, not a policy abstraction.

## The dual goal — two registers, one voice

Every post serves **one primary goal**. They overlap; they are not the same post. Name the register before writing.

### Register A — Peer authority ("I belong among CTOs")
Be read as a credible equal by other (Group) CTOs.
- **Currency:** hard-won architectural judgment, trade-offs I owned, systems I shipped *and killed*, boring-tech-wins convictions, war stories with a real technical spine.
- **Signals:** "I have built and broken real systems under real constraints." Depth, not breadth. A specific decision beats a general principle.
- **Failure mode:** sounding like a vendor or a conference abstract.

### Register B — Board-readiness ("I belong at the table")
Signal board-readiness to appointment decision-makers and sitting board members.
- **Currency:** governance-level judgment — risk, capital allocation, oversight, org scaling, strategic bets, regulatory posture. The **Business → Product → Technology** lens (Soul.md), top-down.
- **Signals:** "I can sit where the money and the risk are decided and challenge the CEO on the tech bet." Outcomes and money first, code last.
- **Failure mode:** a delivery brag dressed as governance — "look what I shipped" is not board judgment.

**The tie-breaker:** if a post could equally be written by a senior engineer, it is Register A at best and probably broadcast. Board posts must carry a judgment only someone who has *owned the P&L consequence of a tech bet* would make.

## The audience-fit test (blunt gate)

Before publishing, answer honestly:

> **Would a European Group CTO or a sitting board member stop, respect this, and think "this person belongs at my level"?**

If the honest answer is "a junior would like it" or "it's a nice general post" — it is not for this ICP. Rewrite or kill.

Five supporting checks — the first two are the original pair; two were added 2026-07-19 for the manufacturing co-primary audience (see `[[career-manufacturing-cto-positioning]]`), and the **methodology test** was added 2026-08-22:
- **Peer test:** could only someone who has actually done this write it? If a smart outsider could, it's too generic.
- **Table test (for Register B):** does it carry a judgment about value, risk, or capital that a board would care about — not just a technical outcome?
- **Manufacturing-buyer test:** would a Head of Digital/Industry 4.0, VP Ops/Manufacturing, plant leader, or industrial-PE operating partner recognise this as *their* problem — capacity, capital, sovereignty, resilience — or does it read as written for IT/software peers?
- **Methodology test (added 2026-08-22):** does this read as an operator saying what broke, or as a consultant presenting a framework? If a reader could reconstruct a stage model from it — numbered stages, the words *maturity / journey / framework*, a diagram that would work as a slide — it fails. Grounded in evidence, not taste: the Company A panel had written *"kein Guru / Umsetzungsverantwortung"* into its briefing and was explicitly testing *"Umsetzer oder Berater?"* before the hearing (`output/20260822-company-a-postmortem.md`). A signature framework is the artifact class of the reading that lost the role. The ladder in [`ladder-joints.md`](ladder-joints.md) is an **internal** diagnostic for exactly this reason.
- **Story-over-pitch test:** does the post open on a real, specific lived moment and let the take emerge from it — or does it announce a capability, a framework, or an event ("come see me speak")? The two 2026-07 posts that scored 0.00% and 0.16% engagement both failed this test — they opened on a generalising capability claim ("Most factories still buy machines…", "Most manufacturers own their capacity…") with no lived moment behind it. If the honest answer is "it's a pitch," rewrite it as the moment that produced the insight, or kill it.

*Confidentiality is absolute (Soul.md inviolable line): GovTech/defense, consortium, and ministry material must be abstracted so no client, programme, or protected detail is exposed. This wins over every other instinct here.*

## The Buyer Lexicon — the words that decide *who* the post reaches

*Added 2026-08-18. This section exists because of a mechanism, not a style preference. LinkedIn's 2026
retrieval layer embeds members and posts into one semantic space, and the member's vector is built from
their **profile, skills, work history, and the posts they've actually engaged with** (see
[`ranking-model-2026.md`](../../../workflow-ops/linkedin-viral-scheduler/references/ranking-model-2026.md)
M4). So the vocabulary a post uses is not decoration on top of the message — it is a large part of
**which neighbourhood the post gets retrieved into.***

This is the concrete lever on the long-standing **Rule 5 gap** (`engagement-learnings.md`): ~10 of 19
posts were on-theme for manufacturing, yet viewers stayed IT-services/consulting/software. Same problem,
described in two different professional dialects. The consultant dialect is the one that was being used.

### The two dialects for the same problem

| The problem | Consultant dialect (lands near consultants) | Buyer dialect (lands near the plant) | **D1 German** (owner-led Mittelstand) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Machines sold once, no recurring revenue | "servitization", "business-model transformation", "value-based pricing" | "the machine leaves the factory and we never see the margin again", "pay-per-part", "who owns the spindle hours" | *Ersatzteilgeschäft, Servicemarge, Händlerkanal, wiederkehrende Erlöse, Maschinenpark* |
| Getting data off the shop floor | "edge-to-cloud data fusion", "IIoT enablement", "data architecture" | "the PLC won't talk to anything", "OPC UA gateway", "the line's data sits in a cabinet nobody's opened since commissioning" | *Steuerung, Schaltschrank, Bestandsanlagen, Nachrüstung, Schnittstelle* |
| Downtime | "operational excellence", "predictive maintenance capability" | "unplanned downtime", "MTBF", "the changeover took the whole shift", "we lost a night of production" | *Stillstand, Rüstzeit, Verfügbarkeit, Instandhaltungsfenster, eine Nachtschicht verloren* |
| Capacity | "capacity optimization", "asset utilization" | "OEE", "we're booking machine hours", "the retooling window", "second-shift capacity" | *Auslastung, Spindelstunden, Kapazität, Engpass, zweite Schicht* |
| Sovereignty | "digital sovereignty", "Gaia-X compliance" | "our OEM sees our recipes", "the data leaves the plant", "one supplier and no second source", "spare-parts lead time out of Asia" | *Rezeptdaten, Datenhoheit, die Daten verlassen das Werk, zweiter Lieferant, Lieferzeit aus Asien* |
| AI on the floor | "agentic AI", "AI transformation" | "does it work when the network drops", "who signs off when it's wrong", "the operator has to trust it at 3am" | *läuft das auch ohne Netz, wer haftet wenn's falsch ist, der Bediener muss dem um 3 Uhr nachts trauen* |

### The three named dialects (added 2026-08-22)
The lexicon is now organised as three dialects rather than one buyer/consultant split, because the campaign targets three classes with one thesis (`output/20260822-ladder-campaign-charter.md` §6).

| Dialect | Buyer | Language | Anchor vocabulary |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 Plant floor** | Owner/CEO, DACH Mittelstand OEM, 500–3,000 FTE | **German** | The right-hand column above — *Spindelstunden, Servicemarge, Verfügbarkeit, Rüstzeit, Ersatzteilgeschäft* |
| **D2 Capital** | Industrial-PE operating partner, roll-up platform | English | EBITDA bridge, capex-to-opex, asset utilisation, recurring contracted share, buy-and-build, carve-out, value-creation plan, exit multiple |
| **D3 Group / consortium** | Division CEO, group CTO, EU programme | English | OPC UA, interoperability, data residency, sovereignty, Gaia-X, standardisation across plants, Digital Product Passport, conformance |

**German is written, not translated.** A translated post sounds translated and this audience hears it in one line. If the argument cannot be made natively in German, it is a D2/D3 post.

**Known tooling limits — measured 2026-08-22 on a real German test draft, not assumed.** A German draft scored `composite 92.5, band "Viral Potential"`, so the gate works. What it gets wrong:

| Signal | German behaviour | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `personal_markers` | **Works** — 5 detected | first-person voice reads correctly |
| `aifluff` | 100, zero phrase hits | German fluff is neither penalised nor **caught**. Human read only |
| `buyer_dialect` | **Works** — matched *Inbetriebnahme*, *Rüstzeit* | the German lexicon is partly in the script already |
| `sovereignty` | **0 on a post explicitly about Datenhoheit** | the detector is English-only, so a German sovereignty post scores zero on the account's highest-converting axis |
| `conversation_trigger` | tension markers 0 | under-reads German tension |

**Do not fix this by fudging weights.** All four affected signals are *reported, non-gating*, so nothing is blocked — the limit is a reporting artifact. But it does mean **cross-language score comparison is invalid**: the H4 German/English matched-pair test must be judged on measured decision-maker density from the export, never on these composites. Extending the German wordlists is a legitimate future change; inventing a German sovereignty score is not.

**Neither dialect is dishonest.** The buyer dialect is simply the one that appears in a plant leader's own
profile and in the posts they engage with — which is exactly what retrieval matches against.

### Working vocabulary by segment

  concentration risk, second source, regulatory exposure, oversight, org scaling, technology bill.

### How to keep this honest and current

Don't invent this list — **harvest it.** Three sources, in order of value:

1. **The vault's own people notes** (`cbrain/Clients/`, `type: person`) — real buyers' titles and the
   words in their own material.
2. **Posts you comment on** ([[linkedin-post-discovery]] + [[linkedin-comment-engagement]]): the buyer's
   phrasing is right there in the post you're replying to. Add any recurring term you didn't have.
3. **The weekly review's audience data** ([[linkedin-weekly-review]]): if the manufacturing share moves
   after posts that used the buyer dialect, that is the first real evidence this lever works — log it in
   `engagement-learnings.md` as measured, not assumed.

### The lexicon check (new gate step, cheap)

Before publishing, answer: **which segment's dialect is this post written in, and name three terms in it
that would appear in that buyer's own profile.** If the three terms are all consultant dialect, the post
will be retrieved to consultants — rewrite in the buyer's words or accept that audience.

*This is a craft lever with a mechanistic basis, and it is still **unmeasured for this account**. Treat
it as a strong hypothesis to be confirmed by the manufacturing-viewer share in the weekly review — not as
a proven result.*
