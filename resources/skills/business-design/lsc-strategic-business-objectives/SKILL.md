# LSC 25 — Strategic Business Objectives (Management Track)

## Contract
- **Input:** strategy documents, customer, product, business, competitor and trend data (including `canvases/24-trends.md`), and management.
- **Output:** `canvases/25-strategic-business-objectives.md`: focus area, data conclusions, two or more candidate objectives with why/why-not, and the chosen one handed to 01.
- **Allowed tools/artifacts:** company data, strategy documents, `opportunity-framing`, `assumption-audit` for the why-not column.
- **Done when:** at least two objective options exist, each has an evidence-backed why and an honest why-not, management and team have both seen the data, and the choice is recorded with its reason.

## Source and scope
From the Handbook 2.0 (Futurice, 2018), Management Track. Not in the 23-page PDF. Purpose in the book: let teams have autonomy by basing decisions on tested facts, not management opinion, and start the design process on facts too. Fields: **What is our strategic focus area?** · **What data tells us** · **Business objective** · **Why should we do this?** · **Why not?**

## Core Blueprint
1. **Focus area.** Name the part of the strategy this tackles, specifically. One focus per sheet.
2. **What the data tells us.** List conclusions from customer, product, business, competitor and trend data (24). Each is a claim with number, source, date, tag. The team working on the topic must be able to see the data, not only the conclusion.
3. **Generate several objectives.** Write at least two, ideally three, initiatives that could fix the problem or use the momentum. A single option suggests the answer came before the analysis; the book says to check that. Every objective is solution-free: it states a result and leaves the means open.
4. **Why should we do this.** Per objective: the strategic fit and whether it enables fast learning with modest resources.
5. **Why not.** Per objective, try to prove yourself wrong: learning cycle too long, out of reach, poor fit with team know-how, cannibalises the core. Use `assumption-audit` to find the weakest claim.
6. **Choose.** With management, pick one objective, record the reason and the ones parked. The chosen one is copied into 01 as the business objective.
7. **Record the fact basis** in `EVIDENCE.md` so the team and management share the same ground (the book's transparency point).

## Quality tests
- At least two objectives; each solution-free.
- Every data conclusion has source, date, tag.
- Each objective has both a why and a why-not with substance.
- Choice recorded with reason; parked options kept.
- Team has seen the data.

## Agent-augmented variant
An agent can collate internal and public data into the conclusions list and propose objectives and why-nots (`[S]`). Management decides. Internal data used by an agent stays inside the confidential boundary (Soul).

## Gotchas
- **Opinion dressed as data.** A conclusion without a source goes to `[I]`.
- **One objective only.** Re-run step 3.
- **Skipping why-not.** The column most often left blank is the one that saves the budget.
