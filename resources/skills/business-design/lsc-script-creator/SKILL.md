# LSC 04 — Script Creator

## Contract
- **Input:** `canvases/03-customer-groups.md` (main group), `canvases/02-immersion.md` (guesses and data gaps).
- **Output:** `canvases/04-script-creator.md` (the canvas) and `interview-script.md` in the same STATE_ROOT, plus a screener and a documentation protocol.
- **Allowed tools/artifacts:** conversation, vault, scheduling tools the user has, `service-discovery-research` for method depth.
- **Done when:** the script has been run once as a dry run, timings noted, revisions applied, and the recruiting screener excludes people outside the main group.

## Canvas (v3.0.5) — "Preparing for the insight interviews."
Callout: *Don't ask what the user wants, try to find out what she needs on the deeper level.*
Top: **What do we need to learn about the interviewee?** (four boxes, each: *How to ask about it in the interview?*)
Middle: **Ask "Tell me about the last time you…" and draw a timeline with the customer.** Draw the journey with her on A3, ask many *why* questions, seek the emotions and values behind the events. Three rows over five phase columns: **1. Make a guess of the phases of the customer's journey related to you** · **2. What do we need to learn about the actions and motives related to this phase?** · **3. What do we need to learn about the emotions and values related to this phase?**
Bottom: **Intro** (introduce yourself and the interview; how answers are handled; basic info; how you know the person belongs to the group; a warm-up about everyday life) · **Thank you** (reward; ending; time-keeping; ending questions: anybody else in your organisation to talk to? OK to interview again?) · **How will you document the interview?** (agree practicalities; always write down what surprised you).

## Core Blueprint
1. **Learning goals.** Pick four things to learn that, if known, would change the concept: from the `[I]` guesses in 02/03 and the data gaps. Write each as a question to yourself.
2. **Guess the journey.** Five phases of the customer's journey related to the topic, from the trigger to the aftermath. Label `[I]`; the customer redraws it in the interview.
3. **Per phase:** write what to learn about actions and motives (row 2) and emotions and values (row 3).
4. **Convert to questions.** Anchor in the last concrete time ("Tell me about the last time you…"), never hypotheticals ("Would you…"). Follow-ups: "What happened next?", "Why?", "How did that feel?", "What did you do instead?". No leading questions, no pitching.
5. **Intro and close.** Complete the Intro and Thank-you lists from the canvas. State data handling in one sentence: who sees notes, how long they are kept, that quotes are anonymised. Use a consent line appropriate to the jurisdiction (GDPR in the EU).
6. **Documentation.** Two people per interview where possible: one asks, one notes. Verbatim quotes in quotation marks; observations separate; a **Surprise log** per interview. Record only with consent.
7. **Dry run** on a colleague outside the project. Cut questions that take more than a minute to explain. Target 45–60 minutes. **[house]**
8. **Plan the number.** Start with five per main group, then continue until new interviews stop producing new needs. Name this as a working bet, not a statistical guarantee. **[house]**

## Quality tests
- Every question is about past behaviour or a concrete event, none starts "Would you use…".
- Each of the four learning goals maps to at least two questions.
- Each journey phase has an action/motive probe and an emotion/value probe.
- Intro contains data-handling and consent; close contains both ending questions.
- Screener asks for behaviour, not for interest in our idea.
- Dry run done and timed.

## Agent-augmented variant
Agents may critique the script for leading questions and draft the screener (`[S]`). Synthetic personas may rehearse objections to improve the script; their answers are never `[O]` and never enter 05. Live transcription is fine with consent; the transcript, not the agent summary, is the evidence of record.

## Handbook 2.0 guidance (book, Futurice 2018, paraphrased)
Handbook interviewing cheat sheet (paraphrased):
- **Problem interview first, solution interview later.** Never present your solution in a problem interview.
- Warm-up: introduce everyone and their roles, the theme, what happens to answers, that criticism is welcome, that the interviewee is the expert, then easy questions (job, place, household).
- Ask for concrete examples ("tell me about the last time you…"). Open questions with why, how, what. Interviewee talks about 80%, you about 20%.
- Traps: people want to please you. Do not reveal what answer you hope for, do not generalise, leave a few seconds of silence, act naive and ask why.
- Ask why repeatedly and ask for drawings. Start with five interviews.
- Ending: thank them, ask permission for a follow-up and for referrals, and write down the three most interesting findings immediately.

## Gotchas
- **Asking what they want.** The canvas's own callout. Redirect to the last time.
- **Fishing for validation of the idea.** Interviews here discover needs; concept tests belong to 15 and 21.
- **No surprise log.** If nothing surprised you, you confirmed your own guesses.
