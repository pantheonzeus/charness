# Lean Service Creation Handbook 2.0 — what the book adds (paraphrased)

Source: *Lean Service Creation: Change Agents' Open Source Tools for the what, the how and the why*, Marjaana Toiminen with Hanno Nevanlinna and Risto Sarvas, Futurice Oy, Helsinki, April 2018 (ISBN 978-952-94-0381-3). The principal supplied the PDF. No license is stated in the text beyond calling LSC open source; this file paraphrases and does not reproduce passages. Page references are PDF page numbers.

## 1. The five principles (manifesto, pp. 35-45)
1. **All for the team, team for all.** Multidisciplinary teams that care for and trust each other; hierarchies and closed groups hurt creativity; stay open to new people.
2. **Love the problem, not the solution.** Fall in love with the customer's and the organisation's problems and change solutions fast.
3. **Be transparent no matter what you do.** Make abstract things tangible, visualise dependencies and decisions so others can contribute.
4. **Never stop iterating, never stop learning.** Demand broad, clear goals that leave room for iteration. A detailed project plan is the enemy of iteration.
5. **See the forest, spot the squirrel.** Tie details to the bigger objective so strategy and implementation connect.

## 2. What LSC is (pp. 20-24, 111)
- Lean Startup supplies constant assumption validation, design thinking supplies depth on customer needs, Agile supplies focused cross-functional teams. LSC adds business orientation: never lose sight of the business objective.
- It works as a tool set, as actions and behaviour, as thinking, as a shared language across disciplines, and as company culture. A shared language stops people hiding behind jargon.
- The canvases are posters on a wall, a checklist to see the service from all angles, a visible way to ask for feedback, and a way to teach how new business is created.
- Origin: 2013, a client asked Futurice to teach them how to create services instead of delivering an app.
- Used in many sectors including utilities, telecom, marine and energy technology, finance, retail, public bodies and universities (Telia, Wärtsilä, Helen, Vapo, Posti and others named in the book).

## 3. Structure of the handbook (pp. 114-117)
Tools for teams' daily work: Weekly :), Weekly LSC Board, Agile Board, Retrospective, Retro Diamonds, Experimenting. Management track (pre-phase): Trends, Strategic Business Objectives, Success is a Process. Service Vision Sprint: Business Objective & Context, Immersion, Customer Grouping, Insight, Ideation Kick Off, Concept and Value Proposition, Profiling the Concept, Customer Engagement, Business Model & Market Size, Validation. Defining and building the MVP: Minimum Viable Lovable Product, MVP Backlog, What to Measure. Growing it huge: Growth Hacking. Iterate until the success criteria from management are passed. For bigger feature changes later, reuse the relevant Service Vision Sprint canvases.

## 4. Lessons from the cases and interviews
- **Helen (energy):** engineers default to their own knowledge; talking to customers meant a change of principles. Calendars booked months ahead block lean work. After the programme, support for continuing varied hugely by department. Decide whether to build with customers or to polish slides for executives outside the process. Fake ad reused as a commitment test (about 2,000 interested buyers for solar panels, with openly stated non-existence).
- **Telia:** a one-day crash course only made sense once applied to a real project. Inner-startup model with steering group and regular investor meetings; pivots by mutual decision. Demo phase run like a game with votes. After the canvases the hard question was how to organise development; the team sliced each key finding into a separate development item. Teams later ran LSC themselves, with around ten programmes at once. Benefit named: all teams work the same way, which brings clarity and transparency. They also pull a single canvas for a single question.
- **Vapo:** executives must grasp what is being done and to what end. Engineers can solve problems well but must question the problem. The risk: will it stick, or will the company slide back to business plan, budget and decision. The writer argues continuous small tests minimise risk better than waterfall.
- **Wärtsilä (Marco Ryan):** works only with management support: dedicated time, permission to fail, leaders who call it a learning process. It can reduce resistance to collaboration and communication.
- **Fira (Jussi Aho):** define must-win battles and align the organisation; KPIs depend on whether you aim at incremental change or a breakthrough; think from the future back to the present.
- **OP Labs (Kristian Luoma):** tools do not free you from thinking; the why matters most; turn guesses into facts.
- **Futurice voices:** lean must not replace thinking (Tuomi); failing fast is not killing projects fast; the process makes you validate ideas from all angles (Pesonen); LSC is a movement and "lean change enablement" more than a method (Oswald); it is no silver bullet and the organisation itself must change (Raita); the new leader role is to say what, not how, and to give trust and give up power (Nevanlinna).
- **Coaching advice:** the most successful projects start with coaching management on the process, the tools and the values of autonomy and transparency. Teams of the client's own staff guided by Futurice, or mixed teams, both occur.
