# LSC 08 — Rational Concept Sheet

## Contract
- **Input:** `canvases/07-idea-accelerator.md` (accelerated idea), `canvases/05-insight.md`, `canvases/02-immersion.md` (alternatives), `canvases/01-…`.
- **Output:** `canvases/08-rational-concept-sheet.md`, one page, plus the value proposition sentence.
- **Allowed tools/artifacts:** conversation, upstream canvases, `value-proposition-design` for a full jobs/pains/gains pass.
- **Done when:** every field is filled, the value proposition sentence names a need from 05, and the differentiation claim names the alternative it beats from 02 and on what dimension.

## Canvas (v3.0.5) — "Draft a concept out of your idea"
**Concept name?** · **How does it work?** · **Value to the end-user?** (callout: LOVE THE PROBLEM NOT THE SOLUTION) · **What differentiates it from other solutions to the same problem?** · **Value to our business?** · **Social and environmental impact?** · **Rational value proposition:** **END USER:** … **NEED:** … **SOLUTION:** …

## Core Blueprint
1. **Name** the concept with a working name that describes the benefit, not the technology. Naming for the market comes later.
2. **How it works** as a short sequence a customer would recognise (4–7 steps, user perspective first, then what happens behind the scenes). Mark any step that depends on an unproven technology or partner for 13.
3. **Value to the end user**, expressed as outcomes (time, money, risk, effort), each linked to a selected need in 05. Numbers carry a tag.
4. **Differentiation.** Name the closest alternative from 02 (including do-nothing). State the dimension where we are better and by how much; state one dimension where we are not. **[house]**
5. **Value to our business**, linked to the 01 goals: revenue, cost, position, learning. Put numbers in 12, not here.
6. **Social and environmental impact:** copy the summary from 07 lane 4; 10 does the full analysis.
7. **Rational value proposition.** Complete: "For **<end user>** who needs a way to **<need from 05>**, our **<solution in one phrase>**…" Check that the need is a need, not a feature, and that the end user is a group from 03.
8. **Cross-check with `value-proposition-design`** if the concept will be pitched externally: jobs, pains and gains with fit test.

## Quality tests
- Concept name contains no technology term.
- "How it works" can be read aloud to a customer in under a minute.
- Every user value maps to a need id from 05.
- Differentiation compares to a named alternative and states one honest weakness.
- Value proposition sentence has all three parts and no adjective like "innovative", "seamless", "best-in-class".

## Agent-augmented variant
An agent may draft the sheet from upstream canvases (`[S]`) and run a "swap test": replace our name with a competitor's; if the sheet still reads true, differentiation is empty. Sheet content that cites numbers must carry E-ids.

## Handbook 2.0 guidance (book, Futurice 2018, paraphrased)
Handbook "Concept and Value Proposition": select the best ideas from all three idea groups and form a full concept. Several concepts are fine. Reread the business objective in 01 while forming it. Give each concept a name so the group can discuss it.

## Gotchas
- **Solution-first value proposition.** Start from the need, end with the solution.
- **Differentiation by adjectives.** Replace with a measured difference against a named alternative.
- **Hiding the dependency.** A step that needs a partner or new technology goes into 13 now, not at the last gate.
